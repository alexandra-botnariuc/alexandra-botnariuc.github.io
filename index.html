01 — About

Who I Am

I'm a PhD candidate at Leibniz University Hannover, employed by the Max Planck Institute for Gravitational Physics (AEI) in Hannover, where I work in the Pulsar Group led by Dr. Colin Clark. My research focuses on discovering new radio pulsars and compact binaries in major survey datasets — working with data from PALFA (Arecibo), the FAST GPPS survey, the Parkes Multibeam Pulsar Survey, and the MeerKAT Galactic Centre search. I also observe actively with the Parkes radio telescope for discovery follow-up and timing.

My background spans Cambridge physics, professional software development (including a team lead role and Symfony certification), an MSc in computational science, and bioinformatics research. That combination means I approach pulsar searching as both an astronomer and an engineer: I build the pipelines, develop the algorithms, and run them on GPU and CPU clusters at scale.

My longer-term research goal is to develop a dedicated programme on pulsar planets. Key open questions remain largely unanswered: how and why did these systems form? Did the planets survive the supernova, or form from post-explosion debris? Could any of them be habitable in some sense? What do they tell us about planetary formation channels more broadly, and which assumptions from standard planet formation theory break down in this regime? I aim to build the observational and theoretical foundation needed to tackle these questions, and to pursue exoplanet expertise as a bridge toward a future ERC-funded programme in this area.

02 — Research

Research Interests

Astroinformatics & Survey Searches
Large-scale pulsar survey pipelines across PALFA, FAST GPPS, PMPS, and MeerKAT. Custom clustering algorithms and ML-based candidate classification on GPU/CPU clusters.
Compact Binary Searches
Targeted acceleration searches for tight-orbit binary radio pulsars — computationally the hardest problem in pulsar astronomy, requiring jerk searches and GPU-accelerated pipelines.
Radio Observations
Discovery follow-up and precision timing of new pulsars with Parkes/Murriyang. Observational campaigns bridging survey discoveries to full timing solutions.
Pulsar Planets
Timing residual searches for planetary companions, formation theory, and ALMA observational constraints on post-supernova debris disks — a long-term ERC target.
Binary Evolution
Recycling scenarios, mass transfer, and the formation of exotic compact binaries. Bridging observational search results to population synthesis models.
Spider Pulsars
Black widow and redback MSPs ablating their companions in tight orbits — characterisation, orbital dynamics, and multi-wavelength follow-up.
